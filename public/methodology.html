AI Capability Trade-off Model

A reduced-form specification, and a record of how much of it this calculator computes.

Research question

How does reallocating a share of frontier AI R&D compute from capability research to alignment research affect frontier AI capability and AI safety over time?

Note Both channels are implemented. They do not rest on equal footing: the capability channel follows the specification, while the safety channel supplies its own baseline and prior for λ, neither of which the source fixes.

I Implemented

Compute reallocation

Let CR(t) be total frontier AI R&D compute and x the share of it reallocated to alignment. Under the policy the R&D budget splits in two: the remaining capability-oriented compute, and the alignment compute it paid for.

CC,P(t) = (1 − x)CR(t)(1.1)
CA,P(t) = x CR(t)(1.2)

Under the baseline nothing is reallocated, so the whole budget stays with capability work.

CC,B(t) = CR(t)(1.3)
CA,B(t) = 0(1.4)
Unused term Equation (1.2) is specified but has no consumer. Alignment compute only enters the model through Parts IV and V, so nothing downstream of the calculator reads it.

The calculator implements (1.1), (1.3) and (1.4). It carries no quantity derived from (1.2).

II Implemented

Software progress

Let S(t) denote the software (algorithmic) progress index. Software progress follows an ideas-production function, run once for the baseline and once for the policy.

ṠB(t) = A[CR(t)]β[SB(t)]γ(2.1)
ṠP(t) = A[(1 − x)CR(t)]β[SP(t)]γ(2.2)

Both arms start from the same initial condition.

SB(0) = SP(0) = S0(2.3)
A
Research productivity.
β
Elasticity of software progress with respect to capability R&D compute.
γ
Software feedback — knowledge accumulation and AI-assisted R&D.

The policy enters through one factor. Comparing (2.2) with (2.1), reallocation multiplies the instantaneous rate by (1 − x)β and changes nothing else.

III Implemented

Frontier AI capability

Frontier AI capability is modeled using a reduced-form Cobb–Douglas production function over training compute and software.

Mj(t) = [CT(t)]α[Sj(t)]δ,  j ∈ {B, P}(3.1)
M(t)
Frontier AI capability index.
CT(t)
Frontier training compute.
α
Training-compute elasticity.
δ
Software elasticity.

Since the policy reallocates only R&D compute, training compute is identical in both arms.

CT,B(t) = CT,P(t) = CT(t)(3.2)

Capability cost is therefore the shortfall of the policy capability path against the baseline,

CC(t) = 1 − MP(t) / MB(t)(3.3)
Named CG in the interface The chart headed “AI capability gap” plots (3.4) exactly. CG and CC are the same quantity under two names; the parameter contract lists it as CG.

or equivalently, once the common training-compute term cancels,

CC(t) = 1 − (SP(t) / SB(t))δ(3.4)

The cancellation in (3.4) is why α never needs a value. It is a real parameter of the model that the relative outputs are insensitive to, not an omission.

IV Implemented

AI risk

Rather than explicitly modeling alignment progress, AI risk is represented using a reduced-form function. Under the policy, risk decays away from its baseline path in proportion to the reallocated share and the time elapsed since the policy began.

RP(t) = RB(t) exp[−λzx(t − t0)](4.1)
R(t)
AI risk index.
λz
Alignment effectiveness under policy impact level z.
t0
Policy implementation year.
On different footing Parts I–III implement a specification. This part implements a reading of one, because the source fixes neither RB(t) nor bounds for λ.

The specification supplies no shape for RB(t) and no empirical bounds for λ. The implementation therefore holds baseline risk at a normalized index of 100 and draws λ from an explicit working prior, λ ~ Triangular(0.20, 0.40, 0.60). Fig. 5 is a sensitivity surface over that assumption, not a risk forecast.

V Implemented

Safety benefit

Safety benefit is defined as the percentage reduction in AI risk relative to the baseline.

SB(t) = 1 − RP(t) / RB(t)(5.1)
Notation collision SB(t) in Part II is baseline software. SB(t) here is safety benefit. Both are reproduced as the specification writes them.

Substituting the risk function (4.1), the baseline path divides out,

SB(t) = 1 − e−λzx(t − t0)(5.2)

Thus increasing x increases safety benefit, and increasing λ makes alignment compute more effective. Both follow from (5.2) alone. Note what (5.2) means for the calculator: the baseline path divides out entirely, so Fig. 6 is independent of the assumed shape of RB(t) and depends only on λ, x and elapsed time. The normalization in Part IV constrains Fig. 5; it does not constrain this one.

VI Implemented

User inputs

Users choose only two inputs.

  1. Compute reallocation share The share x of frontier AI R&D compute reallocated to alignment. The calculator exposes it as a slider from 0% to 50%.
  2. Policy impact level A categorical selection, z ∈ {Lower, Central, Higher}, which selects a parameter set.
z ⟶ (β, γ, δ, λ)(6.1)
Labels differ The specification writes z ∈ {Lower, Central, Higher}. The control is labelled Low, Medium and High. Same three levels, and the same order.

The mapping in (6.1) carries four parameters, and the shipped bundles carry all four. β, γ and δ are drawn from the source's own triangular ranges; λ is drawn the same way, but from a range this implementation chose, because the source supplies none.

VII Implemented

Calibration of the bundles

The parameter sets are obtained from 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations using triangular distributions. Each parameter is drawn independently, sorted, and read at the percentile its impact level names.

Assumed, not measured These bounds and modes are the modelling team's working choices. No validated empirical calibration exists yet, and the interface must not present these as estimates.
Samples
10,000 per parameter
Seed
Seed 20260731
Sampling
Independent draws
Quantiles
Linear at (n − 1)q

The bundles rank-align each parameter's marginal percentile. They are sensitivity cases, not joint outcome percentiles or empirical estimates. Seeded calibration makes the same z selection reproduce the same parameter set on every run.

VIII Implemented

Normalization and scope

Read as divergence Every output on the calculator is a comparison between two arms of the same normalized run. None of them is a dated forecast of absolute capability.

Software begins at index 100. The baseline is normalized to index 200 in 2034, so the charts communicate policy divergence—not a dated forecast of absolute capability. The normalization absorbs A; unchanged training compute makes α cancel from relative outputs.

The horizon runs from 2026 to 2034 at quarterly resolution. Because the normalization fixes the baseline endpoint, A and CR(t) never appear as numbers: the calculator solves (2.1) for the growth constant that reaches the fixed endpoint, then applies the policy factor (1 − x)β from (2.2) to obtain the policy arm.
